Step one

Three ways to start

  1. No account needed

    Open your web browser and go to google.com/ai. Type your question, or tap the microphone and say it.

    Open google.com/ai

  2. On the web

    Go to chatgpt.com or gemini.google.com to try them without an account, or claude.ai with a free account. Works on a phone or a laptop.

  3. Get the app

    Search the App Store or Google Play for ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. Check that the maker is OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google, because there are copycats. Choose “Continue with Google” or “Continue with Apple” for one less password.

Why make an account?

It’s free. It saves your chats, and it turns on voice and photos.

AI is probably already on your phone

  • iPhone: the new Siri arrived with iOS 27 in September, on iPhone 15 Pro and newer. It’s built with Google’s Gemini models.
  • Android: Gemini replaced Google Assistant in September. Say “Hey Google” or hold the power button.
  • Also: Meta AI in WhatsApp, Facebook, and Instagram; Copilot in Windows and Microsoft Office; Alexa+ on Echo speakers.

If it isn’t working

  • Not what you wanted? Add detail and ask again.
  • Too long? “Give me the short version.”
  • Too confusing? “Explain it like I’m new to this.”
  • Lost? Start a new chat. You can’t break anything.

No typing needed

Talk to it out loud

Voice is free in ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini. It’s a real help if typing is hard on your hands or eyes, or when your hands are busy cooking.

  1. Open the app

    ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. Voice works best in the app.

  2. Tap the voice button

    It looks like sound waves, next to the typing box. In Gemini, it’s called Live.

  3. Allow the microphone

    Your phone asks the first time only. Tap Allow.

  4. Just talk

    No special words. Interrupt any time. Tap the X or End when you’re done.

Tips

  • A quiet room works best.
  • It talks out loud, so use earbuds for privacy.
  • In Gemini Live, turn on the camera and ask about what you’re looking at: a plant, a gadget, a menu.

Try saying

I’m making chicken soup. Walk me through it one step at a time, and wait for me to say “next.”

Ask better, get better

Tips for good questions

DO

  • Give context: who it’s for and why
  • Say what you want back: a list, three sentences, a friendly tone
  • Ask follow-up questions
  • End with “Ask me any questions you need before you start.”
  • Ask “Where did you get that?”

DON’T

  • Share passwords, account numbers, or your Social Security number
  • Paste confidential work into a personal account
  • Trust important facts without checking
  • Give up if the first answer isn’t right

Where AI still stumbles

  • It can sound sure and still be wrong. It sometimes invents facts, quotes, and sources.
  • It tends to agree with you. Ask what’s wrong with your idea, and it will tell you.
  • It only knows your situation if you tell it, so be thoughtful about what you share.
  • It isn’t your doctor, lawyer, or financial advisor. It can help you prepare good questions for them.

On the job

Using AI at work

Good uses

  • First drafts of emails, reports, and proposals
  • Summaries of long documents
  • Meeting notes turned into action items
  • Spreadsheet formulas, explained
  • Rehearsing a hard conversation before you have it

Ground rules

  • Check your workplace’s AI policy first.
  • Use the tools your employer provides, like Microsoft Copilot or Gemini in Google Workspace.
  • Keep client and confidential information out of personal accounts.
  • You’re responsible for what you send. Check facts and numbers.

Staying safe

Scams got smarter, too

The same technology that writes a thank-you note can copy a voice from a few seconds of video. In 2025, Americans 60 and older reported losing $7.7 billion to scams, up 59% in one year (FBI).

If a loved one calls in trouble and needs money

  • Hang up and call them back on a number you already have.
  • Ask for your family safe word. The FBI recommends every family choose one.
  • Check with another family member, even if the caller says to keep it secret.
  • Slow down. Urgency, secrecy, and payment by gift card, crypto, or wire transfer mean scam.

Watch for

  • A call that sounds exactly like someone you love
  • Videos of celebrities or officials selling investments
  • Polished emails and texts with no typos to give them away

Report scams at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and ic3.gov.

Protect your privacy

  • Keep private numbers private: passwords, account numbers, and your Social Security number.
  • Your chats may be used to train the AI. You can turn that off (below).
  • Use a private chat for sensitive questions. It isn’t saved to your history.
  • Think before you connect your email or calendar, or let AI act for you.
  • Covering a photo? Hide account numbers before you take the picture.
