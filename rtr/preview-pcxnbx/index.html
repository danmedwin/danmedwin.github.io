לִמּוּד Learning

Jacob’s dream, from words to pictures

Every translation of Jacob’s dream (Genesis 28:10–18) makes choices a painter has to make too: one stone or many, a ladder or a stairway, God above the ladder or beside Jacob. I used an AI image model to make those choices visible, in a slide and the full study behind it. The third page is a gallery of the dream in eighteen art styles.

The slide

Jacob’s Ladder סולם יעקב

A daf (a page of Talmud) puts one text in the middle and its commentaries around it. I built this slide the same way: the verses in the center, surrounded by six key phrases, how eleven translations render each one, and the AI images each wording produced.

The full study

Eleven Ladders

I sent Jacob’s dream to an AI image model in the Hebrew and eleven translations, tried again with new instructions and with midrash (rabbinic interpretation) as the prompt, and set the results beside seven centuries of real art. Words for physical things steered the picture. Words for God did not, until one added instruction changed it. A chevrutah (paired study) lab lets pairs write their own prompts and compare what comes back.

The gallery

Jacob’s Dream

I made this gallery of Genesis 28:11–12 in eighteen art styles, from a Sephardic illuminated manuscript to claymation, each with an eight-second looping animation. Following Jewish practice, none of the images show God. Each stairway rises into sky and light, and the angels (malachim, a word that also means messengers) appear as flames, birds, and light.
